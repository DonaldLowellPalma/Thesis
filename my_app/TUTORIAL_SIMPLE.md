WaterGuard App Tutorial

Welcome to WaterGuard! This tutorial will guide you step by step on how to use the app to monitor your water quality in real-time.

Lets Get Started

When you first open the WaterGuard app, you will see a welcome screen with two main options: Sign Up and Login. If you are a new user, tap "Sign Up". Fill in your details carefully: enter your full name, your email address (this will become your login ID), create a strong password using a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols, and then re-type the password in the Confirm Password field to ensure they match. Once you tap "Sign Up", your account will be created and you can proceed to log in.

If you already have an account, tap "Login" on the welcome screen. Enter your email address and password, then tap "Login". You will be taken directly to your Dashboard, which serves as the central command center for all your water quality information.

Exploring the Dashboard

Once logged in, the Dashboard gives you an immediate overview of your water quality. At the very top, you will see a text display that predicts whether your water is drinkable with proper treatment, safe for washing only, or not safe for any household use.

Right below the prediction box is your Water Quality Index (WQI), displayed as a score from 0 to 100 along with a descriptive rating. Scores between 76-100 appear in green and mean excellent quality. Scores of 51-75 are light green and considered good. Scores from 26-50 show in yellow as fair, while 0-25 appear in red indicating poor and unsafe water.

The main area of the Dashboard contains four sensor cards showing real-time readings: pH Level, TDS (Total Dissolved Solids), Temperature, and Turbidity. Each card displays the current reading with its unit, and a trend arrow showing whether the value is getting worse (up arrow), improving (down arrow), or remaining stable (side arrow). These readings update in real-time as your sensors collect new data.

Menu Sidebar

At the top right of the screen, you will find the Menu Tab that let you explore different features of the app.

The Dashboard tab (your home screen) provides a quick status check with live sensor readings, the water quality prediction, and the overall WQI score.

The History tab allows you to review past data. Select any sensor (pH, TDS, Temperature, or Turbidity) to view graphs showing values over days, weeks, or months. This helps you understand long-term trends and decide if water quality is improving or declining.

The Notifications tab keeps you informed with all alerts in one place. Critical alerts appear in red, warnings in yellow, and informational messages in blue. Tap any notification to read full details and understand what it means.

The Profile tab lets you manage your account. Here you can view and edit your personal information, change your password, adjust notification preferences and alert thresholds, choose measurement units, and log out when finished.

Understanding the Sensors in Depth

Each sensor monitors a specific aspect of water quality:

pH Level measures the acidity or alkalinity of water based on hydrogen ion concentration. The safe operating range is generally 6.5 to 8.5. Values below 6.5 indicate overly acidic water, which can corrode pipes and plumbing. Values above 8.5 indicate alkaline water that may cause scale buildup.

TDS (Total Dissolved Solids) measures the total concentration of dissolved organic and inorganic substances in water, including minerals, salts (such as calcium, magnesium, sodium, chlorides, and sulfates), and other compounds. The ideal range is generally 50–500 mg/L. Very low TDS (below 50 mg/L) means the water lacks essential minerals. Very high TDS (above 500 mg/L) can contribute to hard water issues and scaling. 

Temperature measures the water temperature in Celsius (°C). The recommended range for most household and non-potable uses is 10 to 25°C. Temperatures outside this range may promote bacterial growth (especially above 25°C) or indicate unusual source conditions.

Turbidity measures the cloudiness or haziness of water caused by suspended particles and sediments. The safe range is 0 to 5 NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity Units), with lower values indicating clearer water. Higher values indicate cloudiness that can shield microorganisms and signal possible contamination or particulates.
